1. Setting and notation

The modelled window starts at $t_0$ (time $0$ in the model) and is split into $K$ slots of length $\delta$ minutes, so the horizon is $T = \delta K$ minutes. The vehicle set $\mathcal J$ is closed: the queue and the plugged-in vehicles at $t_0$ plus every arrival in $[t_0, t_0 + \text{arrival window}]$. All vehicles are in the objective (weight $1$).

Notation.

  • $a_j$ — arrival time (minutes after $t_0$; $0$ for vehicles present at $t_0$); $W_j$ — energy the vehicle needs.
  • $u_{jk}\in\{0,1\}$ — vehicle $j$ occupies a connector in slot $k$; $\eta_{jk}$ — plug-in indicator; $p_{jk}$ — power; $x_{jk}$ — energy delivered before slot $k$ (the recursion (17) runs up to $x_{j,K}$).
  • $S_j$, $D_j$ — start and departure slot boundaries, $D_j = S_j + \sum_k u_{jk}$.
  • $\mathcal J^*{\mathrm{FIX}}$ — vehicles plugged in at $t_0$ whose trajectory is pinned (boundary_mode=FIXED); they are excluded from the completion constraint below.
  • A schedule $\sigma$ is an assignment of all variables. Its value is total sojourn $$f(\sigma) = \sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\bigl(\delta D_j - a_j\bigr),$$ and its makespan is $\mu(\sigma)=\max_j D_j$ (the slot by which the station is empty).
  • A schedule is complete if every vehicle receives its full energy $W_j$. Completeness does not refer to any horizon: a complete schedule may need any number of slots.
  • $E_j$ — any valid lower bound on $D_j$ in every schedule: the solo earliest departure slot of eq. (22) for ordinary vehicles, and $1$ for vehicles plugged in at $t_0$ (they occupy slot $0$).

Two optimization problems with horizon $K$ appear below.

Definition (the two models).

  • Relaxed model $\mathrm{R}(K)$ — the current connector-lane model (Section 6.3): constraints (2)–(19), (24). A vehicle may end the horizon unfinished or even unserved; it then has $D_j = K$. Its optimal value is $r(K)$.
  • Closed model $\mathrm{P}(K)$ — the same model plus the completion constraint (C) of Section 3. Its optimal value is $V(K)$ ($+\infty$ if infeasible).

The quantity we actually want is the closed-system optimum $$z^* = \min\{\, f(\sigma) : \sigma \text{ complete}\,\},$$ the best total sojourn when every vehicle must finish, with no horizon at all. Section 5 shows the minimum exists. Further:

  • $K_{\min}$ — the smallest makespan of any complete schedule;
  • $M^*$ — the smallest makespan among complete schedules with value $z^*$;
  • $M^*{\max}$ — the largest makespan among complete schedules with value $z^*$.

2. Why the current model can "cheat"

In $\mathrm{R}(K)$ a vehicle left unfinished is simply recorded as departing at the horizon and charged $\delta K - a_j$, however much energy it still needs. Not finishing is therefore never penalized beyond that cap, and finishing a vehicle can cost the others more than it saves.

Example. Suppose that in the true optimum vehicle $U$ finishes last at slot $15$, and during its final slots it shares a pile's power with vehicle $V$, slowing $V$ down. Give $\mathrm{R}$ the horizon $K=15$. It can leave $U$ unplugged altogether: $U$ is charged $\delta\cdot 15 - a_U$, exactly its cost in the true optimum, while $V$ now charges at full power and leaves earlier. The model's value is below $z^*$, and its schedule does not serve $U$.

Raising $K$ makes the trick more expensive (each extra slot adds $\delta$ to $U$'s charge), so it stops paying only beyond $15 + (\text{V's gain})/\delta$ — a threshold that depends on the instance and is not known in advance.

So $\mathrm{R}(K)$ is a relaxation of the closed system: its value is a valid lower bound on $z^*$ (Section 6), but its schedules are closed-system schedules only when they happen to finish everyone. That is why the current implementation needed a completion check, a repair step and the closed_* bracket.

3. The completion constraint

Constraint (C) — for the manuscript.

$$x_{j,K} = W_j \qquad \forall\, j \in \mathcal J \setminus \mathcal J^*{\mathrm{FIX}}$$

Every vehicle must have received its full energy requirement by the end of the horizon (slot $K-1$), so no vehicle can be left unfinished or unserved.

Remarks.

  • Departures strictly before $K$ were already forced to be complete by the departure rule (19); (C) closes the two remaining cases, a vehicle charging through slot $K-1$ without finishing, and a vehicle never served.
  • For a vehicle plugged in at $t_0$ the recursion is seeded with the energy it already has, so (C) asks only for the remainder.
  • $\mathcal J^*{\mathrm{FIX}}$ is exempt because its trajectory is pinned to one that did finish in the simulation.
  • With $x_{j,k}\le W_j$ already a variable bound, (C) can equivalently be written $x_{j,K}\ge W_j$.

4. Three lemmas

Throughout, the instance data do not depend on $K$: release slots are fixed, and $K$ is at least the departure slot of every vehicle in $\mathcal J^*{\mathrm{FIX}}$ (true for every horizon used in practice, since $K \ge K_{\text{fifo}}$).

Lemma 0 (the value of a schedule does not depend on $K$). For any feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$, $f(\sigma)$ is the same whatever horizon the solution is embedded in.

The horizon $K$ appears in three places in the model:

  • the start-slot expression $S_j = K(1-v_j) + \sum_k k\,\eta_{jk}$, where $v_j=\sum_k\eta_{jk}$ says whether $j$ is served;
  • the big-$M$ of the lane-sequencing rows (11)–(12);
  • the departure lower bound (24).

Only the first can reach $f$, and only for an unserved vehicle ($v_j=0$). Under (C) every vehicle is served: $W_j\gt 0$ forces some charging, and a vehicle plugged in at $t_0$ has $u_{j0}=1$ fixed. Hence $S_j=\sum_k k\,\eta_{jk}$ and $D_j = S_j + \sum_k u_{jk}$ contain no $K$, and neither does $f$. ∎

(In $\mathrm{R}(K)$ this fails: an unfinished vehicle has $D_j = K$, so its contribution $\delta K - a_j$ moves with $K$. That is exactly why the relaxed model's values shift when the horizon changes.)

Lemma 1 (extension). If $K \le K'$, every feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ extends to a feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K')$ with the same value.

Extend by $u,\eta,p,r = 0$ on the new slots $K,\dots,K'-1$ and $x_{j,k}=W_j$ for $k\gt K$; keep $y$ and $b$. Check each constraint family:

  • Per-slot rows on the new slots (connector capacity, module capacity, station power, (13)): nobody is plugged in and all power is $0$.
  • Energy recursion (17): $x$ is constant on the new slots, so $x_{j,K'} = x_{j,K} = W_j$; (C) holds at $K'$.
  • Taper (18): $p=0$ and $x\le W_j\le R_j$.
  • Departure rule (19) at $k=K$ (now inside the horizon): $x_{j,K} \ge W_j\,(u_{j,K-1}-u_{j,K}) = W_j\,u_{j,K-1}$ holds because $x_{j,K}=W_j$. This is the only step that needs (C): without it, a vehicle charging through slot $K-1$ unfinished would violate this row. For $k\gt K$ the row reads $W_j \ge 0$.
  • Sequencing (11)–(12): active rows are unchanged; inactive rows only loosen (big-$M$ grows from $K$ to $K'$).
  • (24): a valid inequality for every complete schedule (no vehicle departs before its solo earliest slot).

By Lemma 0 the value is unchanged. ∎

Lemma 2 (restriction). If a feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K')$ has makespan $\mu\le K \le K'$, dropping slots $K,\dots,K'-1$ gives a feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ with the same value. In particular, every complete schedule with makespan $\le K$ is feasible for $\mathrm{P}(K)$.

For $k\ge K$ every $u_{jk}$ and $\eta_{jk}$ is $0$ (all vehicles have departed), so $p_{jk}=0$ by (13); set the module variables to $0$ there as well. Every remaining row only involves slots $\lt K$ and is unchanged, and $x_{j,K}=x_{j,K'}=W_j$ because no energy is delivered after slot $K-1$. ∎

5. Main theorem: how the optimum depends on the horizon

Let $F = \sum_{i\in\mathcal J}\bigl(\delta E_i - a_i\bigr)$ (the total sojourn if every vehicle left at its earliest possible slot), and for any value $\bar Z$ define

$$K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z) \;=\; \max_{j} E_j \;+\; \Bigl\lceil \frac{\bar Z - F}{\delta} \Bigr\rceil .$$

Theorem.

  1. Feasibility. $\mathrm{P}(K)$ is infeasible if and only if $K \lt K_{\min}$.
  2. Monotonicity. $V(K)$ is non-increasing in $K$.
  3. Existence and a bound on the optimal makespan. $z^*$ is attained, and for the value $\bar Z$ of any complete schedule, every complete schedule with value $\le \bar Z$ has makespan $\le K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$. In particular $M^* \le M^*{\max} \le K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$.
  4. The three regimes. $$V(K) = +\infty \;\;(K \lt K_{\min}),\qquad V(K) \gt z^* \;\;(K_{\min}\le K \lt M^*),\qquad V(K) = z^* \;\;(K \ge M^*).$$
  5. Same schedule, different horizon. A given schedule has the same reported value at every horizon in which it fits. The reported optimal value changes only when the horizon grows enough for a strictly better schedule to fit, and never once $K \ge M^*$.

(a) A solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ is a complete schedule with makespan $\le K$, so $K\ge K_{\min}$. Conversely a complete schedule with makespan $K_{\min}\le K$ is feasible for $\mathrm{P}(K)$ by Lemma 2.

(b) Lemma 1: every solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ is available in $\mathrm{P}(K+1)$ with the same value.

(c) Let $\sigma$ be complete with $f(\sigma)\le\bar Z$. For any vehicle $j$, every other vehicle satisfies $D_i\ge E_i$, so $$\delta D_j - a_j \;=\; f(\sigma) - \sum_{i\ne j}(\delta D_i - a_i) \;\le\; \bar Z - \sum_{i\ne j}(\delta E_i - a_i),$$ which rearranges to $D_j \le E_j + (\bar Z - F)/\delta$. Since $D_j$ is an integer, $D_j \le E_j + \lceil(\bar Z-F)/\delta\rceil \le K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$. So every complete schedule at least as good as $\bar Z$ lives within $K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$ slots. Within a bounded number of slots there are finitely many integer patterns $(u,\eta,y,b,r)$ (occupancy, lanes, order, modules); by Lemma 0 each pattern has a single value, and whether a pattern admits feasible powers and energies is a linear feasibility question. The minimum over the finitely many feasible patterns is $z^*$, so it is attained, and every optimal schedule (in particular those achieving $M^*$ and $M^*{\max}$) has makespan $\le K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$.

(d) Every solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ is complete, so $V(K)\ge z^*$. If $K\ge M^*$, an optimal schedule with makespan $M^*$ is feasible for $\mathrm{P}(K)$ (Lemma 2), so $V(K)\le z^*$. If $K_{\min}\le K \lt M^*$, a solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ with value $z^*$ would be an optimal schedule of makespan $\le K \lt M^*$, contradicting the definition of $M^*$; hence $V(K)\gt z^*$. The case $K\lt K_{\min}$ is (a).

(e) The first sentence is Lemma 0 with Lemma 1. Combined with (b) and (d), $V$ is a non-increasing step function that is constant from $M^*$ on. ∎

What happens to the optimal schedule (not just its value)

Let $\mathcal O^*$ be the set of optimal complete schedules. For $K \ge M^*$, Lemmas 1 and 2 give that the optimal solutions of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ are exactly the members of $\mathcal O^*$ with makespan $\le K$. Hence:

  • for $M^* \le K \lt M^*{\max}$ the value is final, but enlarging $K$ can add further optimal schedules (ties, with later finishing times), so a solver may return a different schedule with the same total; individual vehicles' sojourns may differ, the total and mean cannot;
  • for $K \ge M^*{\max}$ the set of optimal schedules no longer changes at all.

For the experiments only the value matters, so the threshold of interest is $M^*$; $M^*{\max}$ matters only if the specific schedule is reported.

6. The relaxation sandwich

Proposition. For every $K$ with $\mathrm{P}(K)$ feasible, $$r(K) \;\le\; z^* \;\le\; V(K),$$ with $r(K)$ non-decreasing and $V(K)$ non-increasing in $K$. Moreover $r(K) = z^*$ whenever $K \ge K_{\text{cert}}(z^*)$.

$r(K)\le z^*$. Take an optimal complete schedule and cut it at $K$: a vehicle still charging at $K$ keeps its occupancy up to slot $K-1$ and is recorded with $D_j=K$; a vehicle not yet started is left unserved ($D_j=K$). This is feasible for $\mathrm{R}(K)$ (the departure rule has no row at $K$), and every vehicle's recorded departure is no later than its true one, so the value is $\le z^*$.

$r$ non-decreasing. Cutting a solution of $\mathrm{R}(K+1)$ at $K$ in the same way gives a solution of $\mathrm{R}(K)$ whose value is lower by $\delta$ for each vehicle with $D_j=K+1$ and unchanged otherwise, so $r(K)\le r(K+1)$.

$z^*\le V(K)$ is part (d) of the theorem.

$r(K)=z^*$ for large $K$. Let $K \ge K_{\text{cert}}(z^*)$ and let $\sigma$ be any solution of $\mathrm{R}(K)$ with some vehicle $j$ unfinished (so $D_j=K$). Since $D_i\ge E_i$ for all $i$, $$f(\sigma) \;\ge\; (\delta K - a_j) + \sum_{i\ne j}(\delta E_i - a_i) \;\ge\; \delta E_j + (z^*-F) - a_j + F - (\delta E_j - a_j) \;=\; z^*.$$ Solutions with no unfinished vehicle are complete and so cost $\ge z^*$ as well. Hence $r(K)\ge z^*$. ∎

Consequences:

  • Any lower bound for $\mathrm{R}(K)$ (from the compact MILP, B&P or DW run without (C)) is a certified lower bound on $z^*$ at every horizon, however small.
  • Any feasible solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ (run with (C)) is a certified upper bound on $z^*$.
  • If the two meet at some $K$, then $z^*$ is proven there, and $M^* \le K$ (an optimal schedule fits in $K$).

7. Finding the tightest horizon

The tightest horizon after which a larger $T$ changes nothing is $$T^* = \delta M^* \quad\text{(for the optimal value)},\qquad T^*{\max} = \delta M^*{\max} \quad\text{(for the set of optimal schedules)}.$$ Below $M^*$ the optimum is too pessimistic, above it extra slots only enlarge the model. $M^*$ is not known in advance, so the procedure has two parts: first find some horizon that is certified to be $\ge M^*$, then, if desired, shrink it to exactly $M^*$.

7.1 A certificate from any complete schedule

By part (c) of the theorem, $M^* \le K_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z)$ for the value $\bar Z$ of any complete schedule. Using a better schedule gives a smaller bound, so the bound tightens as solvers improve the incumbent.

7.2 A much tighter certificate

The loose step is replacing the other vehicles' total by $\sum_{i\ne j}(\delta E_i - a_i)$, which ignores the fact that they congest each other. A better bound uses the instance without $j$.

Lemma 3. Let $L_{-j}$ be any lower bound on the closed-system optimum of the instance $\mathcal J\setminus\{j\}$ (for example $r_{-j}(K)$, a relaxed-model bound for that instance at any horizon). Then every optimal schedule satisfies $$D_j \;\le\; \Bigl\lfloor \frac{z^* - L_{-j} + a_j}{\delta} \Bigr\rfloor, \qquad\text{so}\qquad M^*{\max} \;\le\; K^*{+}_{\text{cert}}(\bar Z) = \max_j \Bigl\lfloor \frac{\bar Z - L_{-j} + a_j}{\delta} \Bigr\rfloor$$ for the value $\bar Z \ge z^*$ of any complete schedule.

Take an optimal schedule and delete vehicle $j$. The remaining vehicles keep their occupancy, lanes and power; deleting $j$ only frees connectors and modules, so this is a complete schedule for $\mathcal J\setminus\{j\}$ and its value is at least $L_{-j}$. Hence $z^* = (\delta D_j - a_j) + (\text{others}) \ge \delta D_j - a_j + L_{-j}$. Rearrange and use $z^*\le\bar Z$. ∎

Because $L_{-j}$ accounts for the congestion among the other vehicles, $\bar Z - L_{-j}$ is close to the cost of vehicle $j$ itself, and $K^*{+}_{\text{cert}}$ is typically close to the true last departure. It costs one lower-bound computation per vehicle; a DW bound (or the root bound of B&P) without (C) is enough, and only the vehicles that could plausibly finish last need it.

7.3 Certifying a given horizon with the sandwich

At a candidate horizon $K$, solve $\mathrm{P}(K)$ (with (C)) for an upper bound $U$ and $\mathrm{R}(K)$ (without (C)) for a lower bound $L$. If $L \ge U - \varepsilon$, then $z^* = U$ and $M^* \le K$, with no need to know $M^*$. This is the cheapest certificate when it succeeds, and it succeeded at $K=K_{\text{fifo}}$ on the interarrival 15, 20 and 30 trials. It can fail even for $K\ge M^*$ when the relaxation still cheats (Section 2), in which case 7.1/7.2 are the fallback.

7.4 Shrinking to exactly $M^*$ (optional)

Once a horizon $K_c \ge M^*$ is certified and $z^* = V(K_c)$ is known, $M^*$ is the optimal value of a second, lexicographic problem in the same model:

$$M^* \;=\; \min\bigl\{\, \mu \;:\; D_j \le \mu \;\;\forall j,\;\; f \le z^*,\;\; \text{(2)–(19), (24), (C) with horizon } K_c \,\bigr\}.$$

In Gurobi this is a hierarchical objective (minimize $f$ with priority 1, then $\mu$ with priority 0), the same mechanism the code already uses for tie_break, so it is one solve. Replacing "min $\mu$" by "max $\mu$" gives $M^*{\max}$. Alternatively, because $V$ is non-increasing and equals $z^*$ exactly on $[M^*,\infty)$, a binary search on $K\in[K_{\min}, K_c]$ with the question "is there a solution of $\mathrm{P}(K)$ with value $\le z^*$?" (a feasibility problem — use $z^*$ as a cutoff) also finds $M^*$.

This step is only worth doing to understand an instance or to shrink later re-solves; correctness needs only some certified $K\ge M^*$.

7.5 Recommended procedure

  1. Start at $K \leftarrow K_{\text{fifo}}$ (the discretized FIFO schedule is complete and fits, so $\mathrm{P}(K)$ is feasible).
  2. Solve $\mathrm{P}(K)$ with (C): incumbent value $U$ (an upper bound on $z^*$) and its bound $B$ (a lower bound on $V(K)$). Solve or bound $\mathrm{R}(K)$ without (C): lower bound $L$ on $z^*$.
  3. Sandwich test: if $L \ge U - \varepsilon$, stop — $z^* = U$, certified, and $M^*\le K$.
  4. Horizon test: compute $K_c = K^*{+}_{\text{cert}}(U)$ (or $K_{\text{cert}}(U)$ if the per-vehicle bounds are not available). If $K \ge K_c$, stop — $V(K)=z^*$, so $[\max(B,L),\,U]$ brackets $z^*$ (and equals it when the solve is optimal).
  5. Otherwise enlarge the horizon, $K \leftarrow \min\bigl(K_c,\ \lceil (1+s)K \rceil\bigr)$ for a growth factor $s$ (e.g. $0.25$), carry the incumbent over (Lemma 1 keeps it feasible with the same value) and return to step 2.
  6. (Optional) At the certified horizon, run 7.4 to obtain $M^*$ and the tightest $T^* = \delta M^*$.

Termination. $K$ grows every round until it reaches $K_c$, and $K_c$ never grows because $U$ never increases (Lemma 1 carries incumbents forward). So the loop ends after at most $\lceil \log_{1+s}(K_c/K_{\text{fifo}}) \rceil + 1$ rounds, with $K_c$ at most $K_{\text{cert}}$ of the FIFO value.
